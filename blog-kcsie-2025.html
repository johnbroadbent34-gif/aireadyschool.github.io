What KCSIE 2025 says about generative AI — and what it means for your school

JD
John
Deputy Headteacher & Founder
5 min read
September 2025
Safeguarding & Policy

KCSIE 2025 came into effect on 1st September. For the first time, generative AI is explicitly referenced within the statutory safeguarding guidance. Here is what the document actually says, what it requires of school leaders, and — crucially — what it does not say.

Every September, school leaders work through the latest iteration of Keeping Children Safe in Education. Most years the changes are modest. This year, the headline addition for many will be the explicit mention of generative AI — a signal, if one was needed, that AI has moved firmly into the safeguarding conversation.

But before rewriting your entire policy framework, it is worth reading precisely what KCSIE 2025 does and does not require. The language is more measured than the headlines suggest — and the practical implications, while real, are manageable with the right approach.

What does KCSIE 2025 actually say?

The key addition sits at paragraph 143, within the online safety and filtering section of the guidance. It reads:

KCSIE 2025 — Paragraph 143
"The Department has published Generative AI: product safety expectations to support schools to use generative artificial intelligence safely, and explains how filtering and monitoring requirements apply to the use of generative AI in education."

That single paragraph links to the DfE's Generative AI: product safety expectations guidance, published in January 2025. KCSIE now formally signposts schools to that document — making it part of the statutory safeguarding landscape in a way it wasn't before.

It is also worth noting that misinformation, disinformation, and conspiracy theories have been added to the list of online content risks — a development that sits directly alongside the AI agenda, given the role generative AI can play in producing or amplifying misleading content.

What does the DfE's product safety guidance require?

Since KCSIE points schools directly to it, the January 2025 product safety expectations document is now essential reading for any leader overseeing AI tools in school. In summary, the DfE sets out that generative AI products used in educational settings must meet the following standards:

Content filtering
Effectively and reliably prevent access to harmful or inappropriate content by all users — staff and pupils alike.
Activity logging
Maintain robust activity logs — either within the AI product itself or through supplementary monitoring technology.
Security
Be secured against malicious use and protect users from exposure to harm through the platform.
Transparency
Prioritise transparency and child safety in its design, data handling, and decision-making processes.

These are not aspirational standards — they are the baseline the DfE expects AI products to meet before being considered safe for use in schools. This has significant implications for the tools you may already be using.

Key insight

KCSIE does not place sole responsibility on schools to fix gaps in vendors' AI safety features. However, it does assume that schools have already assessed the safeguarding risks of their EdTech — and are using products that meet the required standards. The onus is on leaders to know what their tools do and do not provide.

What this means practically for school leaders

The guidance effectively asks governing bodies and senior leaders to do three things. First, understand the DfE's product safety expectations and what they mean for your current tool set. Second, discuss with your IT leadership which AI tools are in use across school and whether they meet those expectations. Third, integrate AI risk management into your existing safeguarding framework — bridging any gap between what your tools provide and what pupils need.

This is not a request to ban AI tools. It is a request to govern them properly.

Watch out for this

The most common risk right now is staff using consumer AI tools — tools designed for personal use, not education — with school data. These tools are almost certainly not compliant with the product safety expectations. If your staff have not been told which tools are approved and why, this is the place to start.

The staffing dimension KCSIE raises

Paragraph 143 sits within a broader online safety section that makes clear children should be taught how to keep themselves safe online — including in the context of AI. But KCSIE 2025 acknowledges something important: before schools can teach pupils about safe and ethical AI use, staff must understand it themselves.

This is the staff-first principle at the heart of everything we advocate here at AI Ready Schools. Policy without understanding is just paperwork. The safeguarding framework only works if the people operating within it genuinely know what generative AI is, what risks it presents, and how your school's approved tools manage those risks.

A practical action checklist for school leaders

What to do now
Read the DfE's Generative AI: product safety expectationsFamiliarise yourself and your DSL with the January 2025 guidance that KCSIE now formally references.
Audit your current AI tool useDo you know every AI tool being used across your school — by staff and by pupils, in what contexts?
Check your tools against the safety expectationsWork with your IT lead to assess whether approved tools meet the filtering, logging, and security standards the DfE sets out.

Brief your staffEnsure all staff understand your policy and why the boundaries exist — not just what they are.

The bigger picture

KCSIE 2025 is not a dramatic overhaul of the AI landscape in schools — and it was never going to be. Statutory guidance moves slowly and deliberately. What it does do is confirm, officially, that generative AI is now part of the safeguarding conversation and that schools are expected to approach it with the same rigour they bring to any other online safety risk.

For school leaders who have already been working on this — building policy, training staff, choosing compliant tools — KCSIE 2025 validates the direction of travel. For those who have been waiting for the policy landscape to settle before acting, this is the clearest signal yet that the time is now.

The guidance will only become more detailed in future iterations. Getting your foundations right in 2025 will make every subsequent update far easier to absorb.
