The story

Where this all began

Like many school leaders, the first encounter with AI tools was driven by a practical need — an overwhelming workload and a curiosity about whether these new tools could genuinely help.

The early experiments were promising. Time was being saved, tasks were being streamlined. But it didn't take long to spot the dangers — data entering systems outside the school's control, staff unaware of the risks, no policy in place, no oversight.

The response was to course-correct quickly: move to tools within a controlled tenancy, build internal frameworks, and start looking for external guidance to validate the approach. Beyond the DfE guidance, there wasn't much of substance out there for school leaders who wanted to do this properly.

That gap is why AI Ready Schools exists.

The learning curve

The journey every school leader needs to take

Stage 1
Curiosity — trying it out
Most school leaders begin here. Open tools, impressive results, growing excitement. But also growing exposure — data going to unknown destinations, no audit trail, no policy.
Stage 2
The realisation — spotting the risks
The moment you understand what you've been doing and what it means for your school, your data, and your safeguarding obligations. This is where many leaders either stop entirely — or get serious.
Stage 3
Course correction — doing it properly
Moving to approved tools within your tenant, building policy from the ground up, and starting to train staff with honesty about both the potential and the risks of AI.
Stage 4
Confident leadership — embedding AI well
Staff-led, policy-backed, governance-reviewed. AI becomes a normal part of school life — reducing workload, not adding to it. This is where AI Ready Schools helps you get to.
